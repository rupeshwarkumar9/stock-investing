Stock Investing: Selection & KPI Framework

A systematic framework for short-term stock opportunities and long-term compounders, using Morningstar, Value Line and Weiss alongside fundamental analysis.

Prepared October 2026 • Personal investing reference
How to use this report

Use the framework as a research checklist, not as a mechanical buy/sell signal. Compare trends, peers, expectations and valuation; investigate disagreements rather than averaging them away.

Investment architecture

Use two separate systems: short-term (roughly 1–12 months) and long-term (3–10+ years). The core rule is not to let a losing trade become a “long-term investment” after the thesis breaks.

Core financial KPIs

Growth and profitability

  • Revenue growth: YoY, 3/5-year CAGR, organic vs acquisitions, volume vs price, constant currency.
  • Gross margin: (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue. Track pricing power, mix and structural margin direction.
  • Operating/EBIT margin: Operating income / Revenue. Watch operating leverage.
  • EBITDA margin: useful for comparison and debt analysis, but don't confuse EBITDA with cash flow.
  • Net margin: useful but affected by financing, tax and one-offs.

Per-share economics

  • EPS growth: decompose earnings growth versus share-count change.
  • FCF/share: often more investor-relevant than total FCF.
  • Diluted shares: rising share count can offset business growth.

Cash generation

  • FCF = CFO − Capex.
  • FCF margin = FCF / Revenue.
  • FCF conversion = FCF / Net income.
  • Compare Revenue → EBIT → Net Income → CFO → FCF for consistency.

Returns on capital and reinvestment

ROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital

ROIC tells you how much operating profit the business generates per dollar of invested capital. Compare it with WACC.

Incremental ROIC ≈ ΔNOPAT / ΔInvested Capital

Incremental ROIC is particularly useful for judging whether new investment is as productive as the historical business.

Expected growth ≈ ROIC × Reinvestment Rate

A high-ROIC business with a long runway to reinvest can compound much faster than a high-ROIC business with no attractive reinvestment opportunities.

ROE = Net Income / Average Equity

Use DuPont decomposition to distinguish genuinely strong economics from leverage-driven ROE.

Working capital and earnings quality

  • DSO: Average A/R ÷ Revenue × 365.
  • DIO: Average Inventory ÷ COGS × 365.
  • DPO: A/P ÷ COGS × 365.
  • Cash Conversion Cycle: DIO + DSO − DPO.

Investigate cases where receivables or inventory grow materially faster than sales. Also examine the gap between net income and operating cash flow, accruals, capitalization policies and stock-based compensation.

SBC: Track SBC/Revenue and SBC/FCF, and compare reported FCF with dilution. Large SBC can make headline cash generation less valuable to existing shareholders.

Balance sheet and capital allocation

  • Net debt = Debt − Cash.
  • Net Debt/EBITDA: assess leverage in industry context.
  • Interest coverage = EBIT / Interest expense.
  • Review fixed vs floating debt and maturity schedule—not just headline leverage.
  • Assess current/quick ratio where relevant.
  • For buybacks, measure net share reduction and whether management bought at attractive valuations.
  • Evaluate dividends using both earnings payout and FCF payout.

Forward-looking KPIs

  • Management guidance: revenue, EPS, margin, capex, FCF; focus on changes versus prior guidance.
  • Consensus revisions: FY1/FY2 EPS and revenue revisions.
  • Earnings surprise: beat/miss versus consensus, but also check revenue and guidance.
  • Bookings / backlog / book-to-bill: leading indicators in appropriate industries.
  • Market share, TAM and reinvestment runway: assess future growth durability.
  • Management capital allocation: organic investment, M&A, debt reduction, dividends and repurchases.
